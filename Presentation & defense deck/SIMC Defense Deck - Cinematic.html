The Twist — A Fading Rate

Power  becomes Product.

Now the rate fades over time
ε(t)  =  ε0 e−t/t0\varepsilon(t) \;=\; \varepsilon_0\, e^{-t/t_0}ε(t)=ε0​e−t/t0​

The matrix changes every step — but eigenvectors don't depend on ε\varepsilonε. Every M(t)\mathbb M(t)M(t) shares the same eigenbasis.

⇒ the transient mode collects one factor per step
P⃗(t)  =  c1 v1  +  c2 [ ∏s=0t−1(1−ε(s)) ] v2\vec P(t) \;=\; c_1\, v_1 \;+\; c_2\,\Big[\,\textstyle\prod_{s=0}^{t-1}(1-\varepsilon(s))\,\Big]\, v_2P(t)=c1​v1​+c2​[∏s=0t−1​(1−ε(s))]v2​

Constant rate  →  every factor is the same number below 1. The product collapses to 0. Type 1 vanishes.

Fading rate  →  factors climb back toward 1 before the job is finished. The product freezes at a strictly positive value. A tiny permanent population is trapped alive.

RECT2 S2 / POWER METHOD
T2.2 / 16

Multiply repeatedly and normalise

The loudest eigenvalue takes over.

Each step scales component $i$ by $\lambda_i^k$. The biggest magnitude wins; normalising keeps it visible.

Converges to the line through $v_1$ — the $\pm$ matters if $\lambda_1 < 0$.

$x_{k+1} = \frac{Xx_k}{\lVert Xx_k \rVert} \to \pm v_1$
SIMC 10 · DEFENSE
T2.2 / 16
Each step scales component i by λᵢᵏ; the biggest magnitude wins, normalising keeps it visible. It converges to the line through v₁ — the ± matters if λ₁ < 0. Don't derive — one sentence: the off-axis part dies like (λᵢ/λ₁)ᵏ.
RECT2 S3 / CONVERGENCE RATE
T2.3 / 16

Speed is set by your closest rival

The rate is the spectral gap $|\lambda_2 / \lambda_1|$.

$\rho_{k+1} / \rho_k \le |\lambda_2 / \lambda_1|$

Wide gap → fast and noise-robust; narrow gap → slow and fragile.

On Q3 data: $\lambda_1/\lambda_2 \approx 3.5$ → converges in a handful of steps.

SIMC 10 · DEFENSE
T2.3 / 16
Not the average, not the smallest mode — the *second* eigenvalue. Wide gap → fast and noise-robust; narrow gap → slow and fragile. On our Q3 data λ₁/λ₂ ≈ 3.5, so it converges in a handful of steps. ★ non-obvious insight — dwell here.
RECT2 S4 / WHEN IT BREAKS
T2.4 / 16

No gap, no winner

A vanishing gap is a structural failure, not slow convergence.

$X = \text{diag}(1, -1)$

Vector flip-flops between $(a,b)$ and $(a,-b)$ forever

Oscillates · structural ≠ slow · finite precision turns a small gap into a dead gap.

Any gap finer than ~$10^{-16}$ is invisible — behaves like a dead one.

SIMC 10 · DEFENSE
T2.4 / 16
Equal magnitudes → the ratios never decay → nothing to converge to. More iterations can't fix the *absence* of a target. And in floating point, any gap finer than ~10⁻¹⁶ is invisible — a small gap behaves like a dead one. ★ shows you understand failure modes — judge magnet.
RECT2 S5 / SVD BRIDGE
T2.5 / 16

Rectangular data splits into two symmetric matrices

Both share the same nonzero eigenvalues (= $\sigma_i^2$).

$X^T X$ — covariance / feature space ($M \times M$)

$XX^T$ — Gram / sample space ($N \times N$)

Shared $\sigma_i^2$ bridges them.

$X$ ($N \times M$)

↓

PSD → $\sigma = \sqrt{\lambda}$ real

One symmetric fact, two views: same nonzero spectrum. This is what all of Task 3 rides on.

SIMC 10 · DEFENSE
T2.5 / 16
A rectangle has no eigenvalues, so we square it. PSD makes σ = √λ real, and the two squares are two *views of the same structure* — same nonzero spectrum. This single fact is what all of Task 3 rides on. ★ emphasise: this is the load-bearing result.
RECT2 S6 / ALGORITHM CHOICE
T2.6 / 16

Power-iterate the smaller square

13.5× cheaper on our data.

Pseudocode: Power iterate on the smaller square, then $u_1 = Xv_1 / \sigma_1$

Optimal choice
$d = \min(N, M)$

On our data: $N=924 \ll M=3401$ → iterate $XX^T$ (924²), not $X^T X$ (3401²).

SIMC 10 · DEFENSE
T2.6 / 16
Each step costs O(d²), so we pick d = min(N,M) — the smaller square. Then one extra product gives the partner vector, u₁ = Xv₁/σ₁. This is the non-obvious choice in our solution. ★★ the rubric explicitly rewards this — name it as *your* decision.
