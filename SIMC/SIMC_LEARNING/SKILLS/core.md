# Core Skills — What EVERYONE on the Team Must Learn

---

## Math (the bare minimum to model anything)

- **Algebra** — rearranging equations, substitution
- **Functions & Graphs** — reading/sketching linear, exponential, quadratic curves
- **Units & Dimensions** — checking that equations make physical sense
- **Derivatives** — rates of change, slopes, the core language of modelling
- **Integration** — totals from rates, area under curves
- **ODEs (Ordinary Differential Equations)** — dy/dt = something. This is how you translate "the population grows proportional to its size" into math. Almost every SIMC problem becomes an ODE
- **Basic Probability** — P(A), expected value, conditional probability
- **Linear Algebra Basics** — vectors, matrices, solving systems of equations
- **Regression & Curve Fitting** — fitting a line/curve to data points

---

## Python (your computational tool)

- **NumPy** — arrays, vectorized math (replaces slow loops)
- **Matplotlib** — plotting everything. Judges want visuals
- **Euler's Method** — numerically solving ODEs (you've already started this)
- **Basic File I/O** — reading data in, writing results out

---

## Modelling Thinking

- **What a Model Is** — simplification of reality, not a perfect copy
- **Assumptions** — stating what you're ignoring and why
- **Fermi Estimation** — quick sanity checks ("is this answer reasonable?")
- **Sensitivity Analysis** — which parameters matter most? Change them +/-10% and see what happens. Judges expect this

---

## Communication (everyone needs the basics)

- **Report Structure** — Executive Summary → Assumptions → Model → Results → Strengths & Weaknesses
- **Writing About Math** — no orphan equations, define every variable, interpret every result
- **Making Good Plots** — labeled axes with units, legends, clear titles
